# What the atlas is, and the decisions behind it

Published as a private Claude artifact: https://claude.ai/artifact/LUPQgg2w4dGSFdLcmR1a4F
Attach this zip to a new chat to continue work; edit `src/`, then `node build.js`.

## Standing rules
- Keep sources apart and labelled: Qur'an / hadith / early scholars / classical geographers / modern borders / Claude's synthesis / Nawaz's specification.
- Qur'an, translation and tafsir only from quran.ai (Saheeh International; al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabarī on 5:21, Ibn Kathīr on 24:55). Hadith with collection, number and grading (sunnah.com, dorar.net). Leave out anything not verified; see `notes.md`.
- Write "Allah ﷻ" (build.js adds ﷻ automatically after the name).
- Both caliphate outlines are labelled on the page as a thought experiment / specification, not from the sources and not a ruling.

## The outlines
1. **Best-lands outline** (Claude's synthesis, black dotted): the smallest territory holding every land the sources give a status.
2. **Core caliphate, complete** (Nawaz's specification, olive): the whole Arabian Peninsula; Greater Syria; Greater Israel in its widest reading (al-Ṭabarī on 5:21: Euphrates to the ʿArīsh of Egypt); all of Egypt, Sudan, South Sudan, Somalia with Somaliland, Ethiopia, Eritrea, Djibouti; all of Iraq plus Greater Iraq (both readings: Khuzestan and ʿIrāq al-ʿAjam); Cyprus; all of Türkiye including Istanbul; Iran, Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan. About 11.6 million km², 28 states and territories.
   - (2026-10-07, Nawaz's request, from a list of lands Egypt once held) also **Cyrenaica** (Libya: Al Butnan, Al Qubbah, Al Jabal al Akhdar, Al Marj, Benghazi, Ajdabiya), **northern Uganda** (Equatoria), **Crete**, the **Greek Aegean islands** (Notio and Voreio Aigaio) and the **Morea** (Peloponnisos and Dytiki Ellada). The rest of that list was already inside. Ptolemaic, Khedivial and Muhammad Ali-era claims checked against Wikipedia (Muhammad Ali of Egypt; Khedivate of Egypt; Ptolemaic Kingdom); Morea 1825–28 and Equatoria from general knowledge. Pre-Islamic or Ottoman-era Egyptian rule, not caliphal history. Complete is now about 12.0 million km² in 31 states; partly-core states get split rows in the Greater and V2 tables. Also added to the To-Iraq version.
   - (2026-10-07, later) at Nawaz's request **all of Libya and all of Greece** are in the complete version (replacing the Cyrenaica-only and Greek-islands-and-Morea pieces). Complete is now about 13.4 million km² in 31 states; V2 adds 12 lands beyond it (Libya is already in the core; its province history is still shown). Greece left the Greater Caliphate's 'once ruled' Balkans card and Libya left the Maghrib card; both now sit in the core card "Barqah · Ṭarābulus · al-Yūnān" with northern Uganda.
   - (2026-10-09, Version 46, Nawaz's request) **Turkmenistan, Afghanistan and Pakistan**, whole (Pakistan with Azad Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan, as the base map draws it). Complete is now about 15.3 million km² in 34 states. A strip of Turkmenistan on the Amu Darya's right bank (~17,500 km²) lies outside the natural ring. See notes.md.
   - (2026-10-09, Version 48, Nawaz's request) **Tunisia**, whole, also in the To-Iraq version. Complete about 15.5 million km² in 35 states. See notes.md.
3. **To Iraq's border** (toggle): the same, stopping at Iraq's eastern border (no Iran or South Caucasus). About 9.7 million km², 24.
4. **Greater Caliphate** (toggle; formerly "Greater Middle East"), drawn in the core's colour as one territory:
   - the whole core;
   - the Greater Middle East beyond it: the Maghrib (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco, Western Sahara, Mauritania), Central Asia (Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan), Afghanistan and Pakistan (reference map in `reference/`);
   - (added 2026-10-06) the Muslim-majority countries of Africa not already in it, drawn whole: Mali, Niger, Burkina Faso, Chad, Nigeria, Senegal, the Gambia, Guinea-Bissau, Guinea, Sierra Leone, the Comoros. Criterion: Muslims are more than half of the population in the Pew Research Center's 2020 estimates (appendix B of "How the Global Religious Landscape Changed From 2010 to 2020", June 2025). Close calls, marked "sources differ" in the tables: Nigeria (Pew 56%, no recent official count of religion) and Guinea-Bissau (Pew 56%, 2009 census 45%). Not included: Côte d'Ivoire (Pew 46%), Tanzania (30%), Mayotte (a French department) and Zanzibar (part of Tanzania), which are not countries. Eritrea (52%) was already in the core. Pew figures were read through a page-summarising tool, not cell by cell in the PDF;
   - (added 2026-10-06) every other land a caliphate once ruled, **only the part that was ruled**, each labelled on the map with caliphate and dates: Iberia with Gibraltar; Septimania and Fraxinetum (France); Sicily, Apulia, Basilicata, Calabria, southern Sardinia, Malta; Greece; the Ottoman Balkans and Croatia's Slavonia, Lika and Dalmatia; Hungary (less the far west), Slovakia's south and east, Romania, Moldova; southern Ukraine and Crimea; Krasnodar (Kuban, Taman), Azov, southern Dagestan (Derbent); the Sokoto Caliphate (northern Nigeria, SW Niger, Liptako in Burkina Faso, northern Cameroon, Chad's Mayo-Kebbi Ouest, a CAR fringe; now that Nigeria, Niger, Burkina Faso and Chad are drawn whole, only Cameroon and the CAR add land, but the Sokoto label stays); Tibesti and Borkou (Chad, labelled only); Equatoria (northern Uganda); the Delhi Sultanate at its height (Indian states) and Bengal (Bangladesh less the Chittagong Hill Tracts); Kashgaria (Tarim Basin, Xinjiang).
   - (added 2026-10-06, at Nawaz's request, in two rounds) **joined lands**, so the Greater Caliphate is one connected territory by land (islands aside). Round 1, after a screenshot of the western Mediterranean: the rest of Italy (mainland and Sardinia, plus San Marino), Corsica and the Alpes-Maritimes (the Riviera, Nice), and Monaco, so Iberia, Provence, Italy, Sicily, Sardinia and Corsica join up. Round 2, after three screenshots of the Black Sea, the Caspian and the Adriatic: Slovenia (whole), the rest of Croatia, all of Hungary, all of Ukraine (Crimea has its own row), and Russia's Southern and North Caucasian federal districts whole (Adygea, Astrakhan, Volgograd, Kalmykia, Rostov, Dagestan, Ingushetia, Kabardino-Balkaria, Karachay-Cherkessia, North Ossetia, Stavropol, Chechnya; Krasnodar and Crimea were already in). These join Italy to the Balkans, the Balkans and Ukraine to the Kuban, Azov and the Caucasus, and the Caspian's north-west shore to Kazakhstan; the Caspian is now enclosed all round, and the Adygea hole inside Krasnodar is gone. **Rule (Claude's, not the sources')**: whole countries or whole federal districts, only as many as it takes for every land piece to touch the next by land, and no enclosed gaps. Left out on purpose: Austria, Switzerland, the west of Slovakia, the rest of Russia and the rest of France (all open to the outside, not gaps). Checked by counting the separate pieces of the drawn outline: now one large land block plus islands. Sources vs mine: no counted caliphate is known to have ruled the western Mediterranean pieces (Sicily, Bari, Taranto, Calabria, southern Sardinia were ruled; Corsica was left out as unconfirmed). The Balkan and Black Sea pieces were NOT re-researched in round 2: parts may have had an Ottoman or Crimean overlord at some time (general-knowledge guess: parts of the Ukrainian steppe, Circassian and Kabardian lands, the Sisak area of Croatia), unchecked, so all stay in the joined group. Marked everywhere as an addition (hollow key swatch, "Added: joined" tag, own table group and own regional cards, outline labels such as "Italy, joined", "Ukraine, the rest", "Southern Russia, joined"). The states in part ruled (Italy, France, Croatia, Hungary, Ukraine, Russia) keep their ruled rows and the added land sits in separate `_J` rows that are not counted as extra states; Slovenia is a whole new state. Small enclave holes (under 2,500 km²) in the drawn outline are filled. Not in the base map: the Vatican. Generator: the `JOIN` array in `caliph_gen.js` (needs the Russian regions in `admin1_used.geojson`);
   - Counted caliphates: Rashidun, Umayyad, Abbasid (and dynasties formally under it), Fatimid, Córdoba, Almohad, Ottoman from 1517, Sokoto. Left out as disputed (Corsica is now inside only as a joined land, see above): Corsica, Garigliano, Alpine passes, Dar Fertit, DR Congo. Left out as allegiance only: Aceh, Swahili towns of Kenya, Volga Bulgaria.
   - Crimea listed on its own row (annexed by Russia 2014; recognised internationally as Ukraine).
   - About 35.3 million km², 81 states and territories (28 core, 13 Greater Middle East, 11 Muslim-majority Africa, 27 once ruled, 2 joined whole: Slovenia and Andorra). The joined lands are about 1.6 million km² (Italy, all the rest of France with Corsica, Andorra, Austria, Slovenia, Croatia, Hungary, Ukraine and southern Russia). Austria added 2026-10-07 (Ottoman sieges of Vienna 1529 and 1683 and raids were not rule). Greater total now about 35.4 million km², 82 states.
   - (2026-10-07, round 3, Nawaz's request) **all of metropolitan France** is in: Septimania (Narbonne, c. 719/720–759) and Fraxinetum (c. 887–972) stay the ruled part; the rest of France is joined, not ruled (Umayyad armies beaten at Toulouse 721 and Tours–Poitiers 732; Avignon and Arles submitted 734 and were retaken by Charles Martel from 737: raids and short occupations, not rule; checked against Wikipedia, "Umayyad invasion of Gaul"). Andorra added whole so it is not a hole between France and Spain. Ruled parts traced from Natural Earth admin-1 provinces (`admin1_used.geojson`) or drawn areas; approximate.

5. **Complete Caliphate V2** (toggle "Complete V2" / "V2", hash `#outline-v2`; added 2026-10-07 at Nawaz's request): the complete version (item 2) plus every land on the Greater Middle East reference map (`reference/Greater_Middle_East_orthographic.svg`; the file Nawaz sent on 2026-10-07 is byte-identical to it). Beyond the complete version that map adds 13 lands: Morocco, Western Sahara, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, Mauritania, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Afghanistan, Pakistan. About 22.9 million km², 41 states.
   - Each of the 13 is shaded **province by province** (Natural Earth admin-1, 86 units) by the strongest tie it ever had: ruled by a counted caliphate directly; ruled by a dynasty that recognised a caliph (khutbah, coins, investiture); tributary to a caliphate; ruled only by other Islamic states; briefly held or raided only; influence only. Muslims living somewhere, or Islam spreading there, is not counted as rule. Low-confidence units have a dotted edge; every unit has a note with rulers, dates, notes, what could not be checked, and the sources actually opened.
   - Data: `v2_history.json`, made by `tools/make_v2_history.py` from the research files (three research passes on 2026-10-07, Iranica, Britannica, the ICJ 1975 opinion and Wikipedia with its cited scholarship; no classical text read directly). Claude changed 7 of the researchers' calls to follow the rules (listed in `ADJ` in the script and shown on each unit's note). The core lands of the complete version were NOT re-checked.
   - By area of the 13 added lands, the table shows each state's split by category (e.g. Kazakhstan: a third under caliph-recognising dynasties in the south, two thirds only under other Islamic states).

## Map and features
- Mercator sheet 30°W–104°E, 36.5°S–60°N (widened 2026-10-06 so the Greater Caliphate has room around it; the south edge moved to 12.5°S for the Comoros and, in Version 35, to 36.5°S so the whole of Africa down to the Cape sits on the sheet; the Greater Caliphate view still fits to 12.5°S); Natural Earth 1:10m data; shaded relief.
- Views, "Who drew it" source filter, Go-to chips, place search, share links (#outline-greater, #outline-iraq, #st1…, #lim-a…, #fullmap), regional index, four detail maps, light/dark.
- Phones: Layers/Notes sheets; sideways layout; full-screen mode (CSS on iPhone, real full screen elsewhere). Tablets: notes slide in from the right.
- Greater Caliphate view: whole territory with a margin; the key folds away and the discs, cities and classical names wait until you zoom in (k < 0.9).
- Performance: moving layer with overscan; long strokes and paths are cut to the view when zoomed in (see `clipD`, `parseD` in eng_b.js).
- Zoom smoothness (2026-10-06): while a gesture runs the page only moves the layer (CSS transform). The vector map is redrawn when the gesture has been quiet for 260 ms, or earlier, in a lighter form (no engraved water lines or border halos), if the picture has stretched too far or run out of margin; the pace adapts to how long the last redraw took (`redrawMs`). The idle timer checks for late firing and pending input (`isInputPending`) so a slow redraw cannot trigger a cascade. The land outline has three simplification levels by zoom (`LAND_LOD`), and the relief picture is drawn with `image-rendering: pixelated` in the zoom band where the browser's smooth downscale is slowest. Measured only in a slow, software-rendered headless browser; real devices will differ.

## Look
- Title: an illuminated headpiece. The Arabic title sits in a lapis cartouche with gold rules (after sūrah headings in illuminated manuscripts), the English title and tagline below it, inside a frame of eight-point stars; the edition is a small tab on the bottom edge.
- Type: Alegreya (display), Alegreya Sans (reading), Alegreya Sans SC for labels (true small caps, not tracked capitals), Amiri (Arabic), IBM Plex Mono (figures on the map). Country names on the map keep cartographic capitals.
- Reading sections use flat paper cards (6px corners, no shadow); the map and its panels keep raised frames.

- Caliphate outline: two cards side by side, then the wide specification card (text left, table right) on screens over 1100px. Print style hides the map and controls. A skip link jumps to the map.

## Dār al-Amān: names, natural borders, internal lines (2026-10-07, your specification)
- Names (yours, not from the sources): map label DĀR AL-AMĀN with دار الأمان beneath, bold widely spaced capitals over the Red Sea and western Arabia, the largest text on the map, shown whenever your outline is on (any version) up to zoom 2.6; drawn behind other labels, which keep clear of it. Formal title al-Khilāfa al-Amīna (الخلافة الأمينة) in a small lapis cartouche at the head of the key; demonym Amānī (pl. Amāniyyūn) in the key and notes; capital al-Quds (Jerusalem): gold ring and star around the al-Aqṣā marker, with its own note. The atlas's own title cartouche is unchanged.
- Province labels (the classical regions) are smaller, lighter italic; below zoom 0.45 each shows a short tag instead: ŠM, ḤJ, YM, JA, JZ, ʿR, MṢ, NJ, RM, ḤB. No "Wilāyat" prefix: these are the sources' region names, not provinces of the state.
- **Natural borders is a version of the outline** (2026-10-07; redrawn as a ring in Version 37 at Nawaz's request, "replace the existing Natural borders map entirely"): a fifth button beside Complete, Complete V2, To Iraq's border and + Greater Caliphate (`XV = 'nat'`). Its territory is the land inside one closed ring of natural features drawn round the whole Complete outline, clockwise from the Adriatic: Rijeka over Risnjak to the Kupa spring; Kupa, Sava, Danube (Chilia arm) to the Black Sea; across to Anapa; the Greater Caucasus crest to Bazardüzü and the Samur to the Caspian (keeps all of Azerbaijan); across the Caspian to the Garabogaz; the Ustyurt escarpment to the historic Aral shore and along it to the Amu Darya delta; the Amu Darya, Panj and Pamir River to Zorkul and the Pamir knot; the Indus basin rim (Karakoram crest, round Kailash, the Sutlej–Ganges divide) to the Yamuna's source; Yamuna down, Chambal up, Malwa link, Mahi down to the Gulf of Khambhat; by sea to the Tana mouth; Tana, Mount Kenya, Aberdares, Mau, Winam Gulf, Lake Victoria's north shore to Jinja, the Victoria Nile through Kyoga to Lake Albert; the Nile–Congo divide, Mbomou, Ubangi, Congo to the Sangha, Sangha and Mambéré to the Yadé, the Adamawa to the Benue's source; Benue down, Niger up, Fouta Djallon, Bafing and Senegal to the sea; the Atlantic, Mediterranean and Adriatic back to Rijeka. About 25.9 million km² of land in about 67 states and territories. Islands by the median-line rule (named islands fixed as Nawaz listed them). One style for all land stretches; coasts are not redrawn (the base map's coastline is the edge; the territory shows as a stronger wash). The Libya variant, the Indus–Thar outer line and the barrier zones were removed with the old line; `#natural-libya` now opens Natural borders. The ring is checked against the Complete outline: all of it is inside except about 2,000 km² in Uganda south of the last 40 km of the Victoria Nile (reported to Nawaz), plus slivers of a few km² where the two used different border lines.
- Data: `tools/nat_gen.js` writes `src/nat.json`: for `main` and `alt` the line segments (each tagged with its kind and the ring it belongs to; the territory polygons are rebuilt from them at load), km², and a table of states (id, name, km², share). The one inner sea (the Red Sea) is inside the polygon, closed at Bāb al-Mandab, and is not counted in km²; the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman are open coast since Version 35. Bahrain was missing from the first overlay (an island nation wholly inside the Gulf) and is now in. Cyprus is one table row (the base map splits it in three).
- The natural line as an overlay (`natb`, "Natural line" in the second row of the switch) can still be drawn over any other version. Solid where a coast, mountain crest, river, lake shore or desert edge is the border (coasts thin, the rest heavy), dashed for the weaker stretches (the Bafing-to-Bamako crossing, the Chari–Congo watershed in the Central African Republic, the Elgon-to-Turkana link, the Ndoto and Mathews stretch to Mount Kenya, and the short links Lake Albert to the Katonga, the Katonga to Lake Victoria, and Mount Kenya to the Tana), dotted across Bāb al-Mandab and the mouth of the Gulf of Oman; one hatched barrier zone (Gilf Kebir and the Great Sand Sea), drawn only with the Libya variant. Sea chokepoints (Gibraltar, Suez, Bāb al-Mandab, Hormuz, Bosphorus), Derbent as a fortified pass; Khyber and Bolan as outer watch-points with the Greater Caliphate. With Complete V2 the "Libya as western limit" button moves the overlay's west edge to the Libyan Desert and the Gulf of Sirte; with + Greater, a dashed Hindu Kush–Indus–Thar line is added. Pseudo-layers: `natbAny` (overlay or version), `intlAny` (internal lines: overlay, or on with the version unless `INTL_OFF`), `caliphnat`, `caliphnata` (the Libya variant's title) and `caliphnatx` (version labels). The key carries a note that Western Sahara's status is internationally disputed; the states table flags it too.
- Internal lines (`intl`, overlay): Taurus divide (my waypoints), Nile (with the Blue Nile from the full Natural Earth rivers), Tigris and Euphrates highlighted, the Red Sea axis.
- Switch: first row = the five versions; second row "Lines" = Natural line, Internal lines. The layer panel has "Natural line" and "Internal lines". Third group "Map": **Clean map** (Version 36) hides the numbered discs, all dots and other point markers, place names, the key, scale, compass, coordinates, degree labels and the outline's captions while an outline shows. Links: #natural-borders (the version), #natural-libya (the version with the Libya limit), #natural-line (the overlay alone), #internal-lines, #dar-al-aman, #capital.
- The overlays are drawn the first time one is switched on.
