# Settlements on the line: V2 and V2N (Version 106)

Settlements (cities, towns, villages, hamlets, neighbourhoods; rural and urban) that the edge of V2 or V2N cuts in two, with the people on each side.

**How it was found (my method):** population from Meta's High Resolution Settlement Layer (people per ~30 m cell, census-based, about 2015–2020); inhabited cells less than about 90 m apart are counted as one settlement. V2 is taken as meant: every country listed whole is in to its real borders and coasts, other countries are out, and in the partly included countries (Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan, Kazakhstan, China; India in V2N) the drawn line decides. Real borders: Overture Maps country areas (from OpenStreetMap). Names: Overture Maps places (towns, villages, neighbourhoods) inside each settlement. China has no Meta population data: there, Overture building outlines were used instead (no settlement there is cut). Numbers of people are estimates from the grid, not a census. In thickly settled countryside (the Rwandan and Ugandan highlands, the Khorezm and Fergana oases, Punjab) houses run on almost without a break, so one "settlement" can be a whole populated district: there the number on the near side counts the countryside too, and the number on the far side is the better measure of the people cut off. Where a settlement is marked "at least", it reaches beyond the largest window measured (about 80 km across).
